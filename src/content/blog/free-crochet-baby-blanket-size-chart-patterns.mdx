## Introduction

Choosing the right size is one of the first things to consider when crocheting a baby blanket. A small stroller blanket is perfect for outings, while a larger square blanket can become a beautiful keepsake for tummy time, cuddling, or gifting. There isn't one universal baby blanket size, and crochet patterns often vary slightly depending on stitch repeats and yarn weight. For reference, common crochet sizes include loveys around 12 × 12 inches, stroller blankets around 30 × 35 inches, receiving blankets around 40 × 40 inches, and larger baby blankets around 42 × 52 inches.

Below are 8 lovely free crochet baby blanket patterns that show how different finished dimensions can be used when choosing your next project.

---

## Quick Crochet Baby Blanket Size Chart

| Blanket Type | Approximate Size |
| ------------ | ---------------- |
| Lovey        | 12 × 12 in       |
| Small Cradle | 15 × 30 in       |
| Large Cradle | 18 × 33 in       |
| Stroller     | 30 × 35 in       |
| Crib         | 30 × 36 in       |
| Receiving    | 40 × 40 in       |
| Baby Blanket | 42 × 52 in       |

These are useful starting points rather than strict rules. Stitch repeats, borders, yarn thickness, and personal preference can all change the final measurements.

## Final Thoughts on Choosing a Crochet Baby Blanket Size

A crochet baby blanket doesn't have to follow one exact measurement. The best size depends on how you plan to use it. For a portable project, a blanket around **30–35 inches** can be a practical choice. If you want more coverage, moving toward **36–40 inches** gives you a generous baby-sized blanket. Patterns can also be slightly larger or smaller because stitch repeats and borders need to fit the design.

One important point for new parents: loose blankets should **not be placed in a baby's sleep space**, particularly for infants. Crochet blankets are better suited to supervised daytime activities such as stroller rides, cuddling, tummy time, or as treasured keepsakes.

Whichever size you choose, remember that making a crochet baby blanket is about more than matching a number on a chart—it's about creating something soft, personal, and handmade with love.
